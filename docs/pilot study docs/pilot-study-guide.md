## 1. Critical Moment Prompts (5 min — warm-up)

**SAY:** "I'm going to ask about specific moments. Think back and share what comes to mind."

1. "Think of a moment when the tool **helped** you — what happened?"
2. "Think of a moment when you felt **confused or stuck** — what were you trying to do?"
3. "Think of a moment when you needed to **coordinate** with a teammate — how did you do it? Did you use the chat, talk verbally, or something else?"
4. "Was there a moment where you **ignored** something the tool suggested? Why?"

Also probe here:
- Live Share friction ("Any issues with the shared editing environment?")
- Human chat vs. verbal communication ("Did you use the team chat or just talk out loud?")
- Platform issues: latency, bugs ("Did anything feel slow or broken?")

## 2. Redesign Activity — Disney Method (15 min — main event)

> **This is the core of the pilot study.** The survey tells you what's broken. This tells you how to fix it.

### Setup
Assign roles based on what you observed during coding — assign just before starting, don't explain the method in advance:
- **Dreamer** (most vocal/enthusiastic): No constraints. Imagine the ideal AI PM.
- **Realist** (most pragmatic): What's actually buildable? What's the smallest change with the biggest impact?
- **Critic** (most frustrated): What would still fail? What problem doesn't get solved?

### Round 1 — Dreamer (2-3 min)
> "If HelloCigen could do anything to improve today's session, what would it do? Ignore what's technically possible."

### Round 2 — Realist (2-3 min)
> "What from that vision could actually be built? What's the one change that would most improve the experience?"

### Round 3 — Critic (2-3 min)
> "What's still missing or broken even with that change?"

### Round 4 — Open group (5 min)
> "If we were redesigning this platform for the next version, what are the three most important changes to make?"

**Facilitator: write the group's top 3 on paper, visible to all.** These become your "implications for design" section in the thesis.

## 3. Closing (2 min)

- "Would you use this in a real project? **What would have to change first?**" *(conditional — forces them to articulate the gap, not just yes/no)*
- "Anything we didn't cover?"

---

**FACILITATION TIPS:**
- If one person dominates: "That's great — [other name], what was your experience with that?"
- If answers are vague: "Can you give me a specific moment when that happened?"
- If they're too polite: "It's okay to be critical — honest feedback is the most useful thing you can give us"
- If Disney roles feel awkward: introduce them as "for the next 5 minutes, I want each of you to think from a specific angle" — don't use the Disney names if they feel theatrical
